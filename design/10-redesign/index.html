01 · The measurement — one script, both pages

What the pages do, counted

Each number below is measured in the browser from the two files in this folder, in an off-screen frame at 1400 px, and at 390 px where the row says phone. The same function runs on both pages, so a row cannot be good on one side and bad on the other by construction. It is re-measured on every load of this page.

measuring…

What this cannot measure: whether a frightened resident understands the page, how long it takes them, whether the five nav labels match their words, and whether the plain-language warning is right about the water. Those need residents. What it can measure is the distance between a page and the person reading it in the rain, at arm's length, on a phone: contrast, target size, the length of the scroll to the answer, sideways scroll, the number of things that move on their own.

    04 · What did not change

    A redesign is not a rewrite of everything

    Untouched

    The facts, and the words that carry the law

    • Every tide number, cell for cell. Seven days, four waters a day, the spring range, the datum. The redesign's own audit compares the two tables cell by cell: —.
    • The legal identity. TID/2024/1187, the designated area's name, the 4.20 m threshold, the issue time, the review time, and the official PDF, which is still the record.
    • The register where it is legal. “Flood Warning”, “in force”, “Designated Area” are defined terms. Plain language means sentences a person can act on, not a paraphrase of a statutory instrument.
    • The four news items, the seven notices, the three FAQ subjects, the eighteen footer destinations, the opening hours, the address. Nothing was cut because it was boring to lay out.
    • The Authority's name and mark — the mark is a new drawing of the same idea (a meridian and a tide line), at 24 px, one colour.
    Left undone

    What this page is not

    • The address index is three demo rows. The real answer needs the street list in the Designated Areas Order, and a search over it. The lookup shows what the interaction is for; it is not the interaction as shipped.
    • One page out of forty. A portal is a system: the news template, the document pages, the search results and the grant form all carry the same sins. Task 05 is the system; this is one page inside it.
    • The five nav labels came from the questions on this page, not from residents. A card sort with twenty households would test “Flood status” against “Am I at risk?”.
    • No translations. In a designated area where a sixth of households have a first language that is not English, a plain-language English page is step one of a multilingual page, not a substitute for it.
    • No screen-reader walkthrough, no forced-colours pass, no field test in rain. The claims about the phone are measured at 390 px in a browser, not in a car.
    • The “2 minutes” claim about SMS is a design claim about a service, not a measured one. It is the kind of sentence a designer writes and a service owner must delete or prove.

    05 · Method, so the numbers can be checked

    How the table is made

    The script

    This page loads before.html and after.html into two off-screen frames, one 1400 px wide and one 390 px, and runs one function over each document: walk the elements that carry text, resolve the computed foreground and the first non-transparent background behind them, compute the contrast ratio, and count. The same rules for both pages, in the same pass, on every load.

    The rows are the ones that decide whether a page is usable: contrast, the smallest type, target heights, unlabelled controls, landmarks, headings, the tab order, the number of facts that exist only as PDFs, the scroll distance to the answer, and sideways scroll on a phone. The function is case.js in this folder, and the two pages are next to it.

    What I would add next

    A keyboard-only pass, recorded. A reader's linearisation of both pages, read aloud. A forced-colours rendering. A render-cost measure of the two pages on a throttled 3G — where the honest finding is that the before page wins, being one inline file with no webfonts, and the redesign has to earn its 778 KB back by subsetting and preloading. And the tide-table diff as a test in CI, because the rule “the numbers must not move” is only real if something checks it.
