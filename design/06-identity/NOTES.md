# 06 · Logo & icons — **Meridian Hydrographic**

## Review round: the phone width overflowed by 63 px

Measured at 390 the document was 453 px wide. `tools/chain-probe.mjs` prints the ancestor chain of
every element crossing the edge, and turned one vague report ("63 px of sideways scroll") into three
unrelated causes, all of one family — a grid track with no lower bound:

| offender | why | fix |
|---|---|---|
| `.build-notes > div` (114 px) | a `92px 1fr` grid whose label cell is an unbreakable mono string (`r 6 · 12 · 18 · 24`): the row's min-content is 92 px plus the text's min-content, and an `fr` track takes its base size from exactly that | below 620 px the notes stack, label above text, `overflow-wrap: anywhere` |
| `.sizes .r` (368 px) | four fixed 84 px swatch cells plus four gaps is 368 — nine px past a 390 viewport | below 470 px the swatch cells become `minmax(0, 62px)` |
| `.px8` (a 2 px box drawing at 43 px) | the 8 px legibility box had `aspect-ratio: 1` and no width: inside `grid-template-columns: auto 1fr` the track is sized by the item, the item's `svg { width: 100% }` is cyclic against that track, so the box collapsed to 2 px and the 48-unit glyph drew at its intrinsic size | the box is a declared `width: 190px; max-width: 100%`, its svg `width/height: 100%` |

Two of the three are the idiom this folder already uses elsewhere: `repeat(auto-fill,
minmax(min(100%, 88px), 1fr))` on the icon wall, `minmax(0, 1fr)` wherever a track holds text.

The third cause carries a CSS fact worth writing down. The first override went into the
`@media (max-width: 620px)` block at line 68 of `identity.css` and did nothing, because the base
rule `.sizes .r { … }` is declared at line 185. **A media query carries no specificity**: at equal
specificity the later rule wins, so an override written above the rule it is meant to override loses
quietly. The narrow-screen rules now sit after the base rules, and the comment at each one says so.

Measured after the fix: `docW 390` against a 390 viewport, **no element crossing the right edge**
(`tools/overflow.mjs` returns an empty list) — 63 px → 0. Fourteen shots re-taken, all `errs 0`,
`contrast 0`, `unlabelled 0`.

## The brief

A logo and an icon set for one fictional brand, shown as a system: construction,
lockups, clear space, minimum size, monochrome, an icon grid, a set of icons at
several sizes, usage in a real interface, and the misuse examples.

## What is built

`index.html` + `identity.css` + `icons.js` + `identity.js`, plus **shipped
files**: `favicon.svg`, `mark.svg`, `appicon.svg`, `icons.svg` (32 symbols),
generated by `tools/sprite.mjs` from the same data the page renders from — one
source, page and files both.

**Meridian** surveys coastlines and sells the charts. The mark is one ring, open
at the bottom; a horizon at five eighths of the field; a meridian tilted 15°;
one sun in signal red at the crossing. Every point sits on a radius of 6, 12, 18
or 24, on the horizon line, or on the tilt — the construction page shows the
grid under the mark, which is the only honest way to present a construction.

Nine sections: construction, lockups, scale and minimum, one colour, the 24 px
grid, the 32 icons, in use, misuse, files.

## The parts that are decisions, not decoration

- **The ladder is rendered at 1:1.** 256, 96, 48, 32, 24, 16 px in one row, and
  below 16 the mark becomes the **reduced mark**: ring, horizon, meridian, no
  sun. A 5.4-unit dot at stroke width 3 lands under one device pixel at 16 px
  and arrives as a smudge, so the favicon drops it. The favicon on a plate for
  the same reason: a line mark on a dark tab is invisible.
- **16 px is an edit, not a scale.** Each icon carries a `drop16` list — the
  parts that disappear — and the stroke goes from 1.5 to 1.25. The size table
  shows four columns: 24, 20, the 16 px redraw, and **the 24 px drawing shrunk
  to 16, marked wrong**, so the difference is visible rather than asserted.
- **One colour is a test, not a palette.** Eight tiles: paper, sea, ink, etched
  brass, 1-bit on white, 1-bit on black, one tint, and embroidery — where the
  sun's solid disc becomes a ring, because a filled dot at 3 mm of stitching
  fills in as a black hole.
- **The icon set has six rules** (one stroke of 1.5; round caps with a 2 px
  gutter; diagonals in 30° steps; optical, not mathematical, centring — the
  compass rose is drawn 0.25 px over centre; 16 px is a second drawing; an icon
  never carries the only meaning). The last rule is enforced in the interface:
  every icon in the toolbar, the chart list and the phone tab bar has a label.
- **In use is where the identity is judged.** A 28 px survey toolbar with real
  `aria-pressed` tools, a chart list at 20 px, a 390 px tab bar with 44 px
  targets, the favicon in a browser tab at 16 px, and the app icon at 64, 48 and
  32 on light and dark tiles.

## What is still weak

- **The mark is competent, not iconic.** Ring + horizon + sun reads as
  *sunrise* before it reads as *survey*, and the 15° meridian — the one idea
  that makes it a mark about a chart — is the least visible part of it at small
  sizes. A second round would put the observation (the crossing) at the centre
  of the drawing rather than at its bottom.
- **Six of the icons carry the set; the utility half is generic.** `search`,
  `settings`, `trash`, `bell`, `plus`, `close` are the shapes every set uses —
  honest for a product, unremarkable as a showcase.
- **20 px is a scale, not an edit.** Only the 16 px column is redrawn; a set
  this systematic deserves an 18 px cut too.
- The page has no dark theme (the "on ink" tiles exist, the page itself does
  not), no forced-colours pass, and no print stylesheet.
- `favicon.svg` and `appicon.svg` are hand-authored, so they can drift from
  `mark.svg`; the generator should emit them too.
- No `svgo` pass and no raster exports for the store sizes.
- The keyboard letters in the wall's corner are documentation, not bindings:
  this page handles no keys.

# Round 2 — the chart sheet

## What the page was, and what it should have been

The brief is an identity for a **hydrographic survey** firm, and the page documents a mark built
from one circle, one horizon and one rising line. It measured 9.1 colourfulness with **one hue
family**, and it looked like a stationery brochure: cream paper, one blue, one red, panels of
prose, and a 300 px void under the construction diagram.

The subject was already carrying the answer. A chart has a documented visual language — a
graticule, **shallow-water tints that deepen with the seabed**, magenta for an isolated danger,
a red for a buoy, and a fine italic serif for every depth number on the sheet — and none of it
was on the page. The mark's own copy says *"below the line is water (the field), above is sky
(the reading)"*. The page had no water in it. So the colour system was taken from the chart, and
every place a tint appears is a place the chart puts water: the mark's plate, the construction
field under its horizon, the in-use chart excerpt, and one lockup.

## What is built now

- **The masthead is a chart plate.** The mark sits on the depth bands, over the mark's own 24-unit
  graticule; a compass rose built on the mark's radii (12 and 18) and its 15° meridian; depth
  numerals set the way a chart sets them (fine italic serif, tens digit raised); a title block in
  the margin — `SHEET 01 · IDENTITY · SCALE 1:1 · DATUM OSGB36`.
- **The sheet's margin** is a running tick rule down the inside edge, fixed while the content
  scrolls: the frame of the sheet. It is decoration, so `prefers-contrast` and `forced-colors`
  remove it, and it is absent below 980 px where there is no edge to be an edge against.
- **A documented colour section** (`05 Chart colours`): ink, signal red, danger magenta, sea, and
  the five tints with the depth bands they mean — with the rules measured, not asserted.
- **The construction diagram shows its own claim**: a tint band below `y = 30`, under the grid.
- **The in-use chart excerpt** is a chart excerpt now: four depth bands, the seabed track over
  them, sounding numerals, one **magenta diamond** for an isolated danger, and a band label.
- **The void is a scale bar.** The construction card had three hundred pixels of nothing under the
  diagram; a chart's diagram carries a scale bar, which is the honest filler and the thing a reader
  wants.

Two things were tried and undone: the graticule first covered the whole page, which put body prose
on a texture and made the page hum (text sits on paper; the graticule is the masthead and the
margin), and the plate's first tint ran under the wordmark, so the type was on a band (a chart's
type is on the sheet; its colour is the data).

## The measurement caught a lie, and it was the page's own headline claim

`node 06-identity/measure.mjs` checks the promises the page makes, in the browser, from the geometry
the browser was given:

| claim | measured | verdict |
| --- | --- | ---
| every construction point sits on r ∈ {6, 12, 18, 24} | 4/4 circles, radii 6, 12, 18, 24 | true |
| the mark's stroke is 3, the icons' is 1.5 | mark 3, icons 1.5 | true |
| every icon lives in 24 × 24 | 32 icons, one viewBox | true |
| a 2 px gutter, a 20 px live area | 100 boxes, 0 outside | true |
| **16 px is a second drawing, stroke 1.25** | **0.83 device px** at the 16 px scale | **false, and fixed** |
| a tint is data: no word sits on a tint | 0 HTML text nodes on a tint | true |

The fifth row is the finding. The icons scale by 16/24 = 0.667, so the shipped 1.25-unit stroke
rendered as a **0.83 px grey hairline** on a 1× screen — the exact failure the section claims to
design against, in the section that says "16 px is a second drawing, not a scale". The stroke is now
chosen in device pixels: 1.5 units is 1.00 px at 16, 1.25 at 20. The page states the arithmetic.

## Contrast, where the audit cannot see

`tools/shoot.mjs` walks **HTML text nodes**. This page draws charts in SVG, and an SVG `<text>` is
invisible to it. Hand-checking those pairs found the chart excerpt's band label set **white on tint
4 at 2.32:1**. It is ink now, at 7.24:1.

The whole tint table is in `measure.md`, in both directions, and it produced a rule the page states
with its number:

| tint | ink | white | sea-deep |
| --- | --- | --- | ---
| tint 1 `#eaf2f7` | 15.99 | 1.05 | 11.54 |
| tint 2 `#cfe2ee` | 13.60 | 1.23 | 9.81 |
| tint 3 `#aecbe0` | 10.71 | 1.56 | 7.73 |
| tint 4 `#7fa9c6` | 7.24 | 2.32 | 5.22 |
| **tint 5 `#4d7d9e`** | **4.09** | **4.10** | 2.95 |

Tint 5 is the **dead zone**: 4.09:1 for ink and 4.10:1 for white, both under AA. So the rule is
*tint 5 carries no words in either direction* — a rule that only exists because the ramp was
measured rather than picked by eye.

## The liveliness index barely moved, and that is the honest result

Same instrument, the same 14 shots, before and after:

| | round 1 | round 2
| --- | --- | ---
| lively | 28.6 | **29.9**
| colourfulness | 9.6 | 9.9
| hue families (of 12) | 0.9 | **1.2**

A chart's colour is pale by nature — the tints are shallow-water blues at low chroma, and they
occupy plates and excerpts, not the frame — so a page that is 80% prose about a mark moves a
fraction. `02-dashboard` went *down* on this index by removing a rainbow; this page went up by a
point and a half while gaining a documented colour system, a third hue family and a measured
contrast rule. **The index is not the score for this kind of page**; the geometry and contrast
numbers above are.

Audit: 15 shots (a `tints` shot added for the new section), 0 console errors, 0 overflow, 0
unlabelled controls, **16 contrast findings — unchanged from round 1**, all of them the deliberate
brass-plate mock in the one-colour section, where the "defect" is the point.

## Open defects

- The graticule and the margin ticks are CSS backgrounds; a printed sheet loses them.
- The rose sways 3° on a 24 s loop; it is `prefers-reduced-motion`-gated, and it is the page's only
  motion, which is thin for a brand page.
- The plate's subtitle wraps to two lines at 260 px; a single line needs a shorter coordinate.
- The identity has no dark theme, and a survey tablet at night is a real context.
