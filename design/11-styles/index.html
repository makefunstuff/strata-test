One brief, nine visual styles

The same product copy, the same sections, the same order, the same form — set nine times over. The point is not a mood board: it is to see how far a page can be pushed before it stops being usable, and to measure, not assert, that the nine pages are really nine styles and one piece of writing.

The brief, fixed once

One product, one audience, one set of sentences, written before any style was chosen and never edited afterwards. Every page below renders it from content.json through render.js; a style may choose the typeface, the grid, the colour, the motion and the shape of a control, and it may add the words a style needs (a nav label, a section name, a numeral). It may not add, drop, reorder or rewrite a fact. content.json is the contract.

    The nine, one at a time

    Each card states the rules the sheet obeys, then the numbers that fall out of them, then a judgement. The numbers come from measure.mjs: the rendered text of the page, its own CSS, and the screenshot harness's audit of it.

    The fixes, and the audit that could not see one of them

    Two failures the audit caught, put back on purpose in before-fixes.css, and shot with the same harness. The neumorphism pair is a normal result: the idiom is soft grey ink on a soft grey surface, twelve places measured under the ratio a word needs, and every grey had to be darkened. The glassmorphism pair is not: the gradient-clipped headline passed the old audit with zero findings, because the audit read elements, skipped anything with children (so an em in a headline was never read), and parsed a transparent colour as black. The harness now walks text nodes and reports a near-transparent foreground as no colour to measure — which is why the "before" shot reports exactly one finding. The fix was never to satisfy the check; the check was blind.

    The audit had a second blind spot of the same shape, found after the pages were shot: it never asked which type the browser drew. The editorial body asked for Source Serif 4 through a CSS custom property, the browser dropped that declaration as invalid at computed-value time and used its installed serif, and the task shipped screenshots with a Times New Roman body while the pinned faces sat registered and were never fetched. tools/rendered.mjs now records per page the families the browser loaded and the font files it fetched, and the measurement table prints the count. Every one of the nine fetches its pinned faces; every body and headline names one. A screenshot cannot show a missing font to anyone who has not read the page's own <link> tags.

    Is it the same page? Measuring the content

    Two checks, both run over the text a browser has rendered (not the template source, which contains placeholders). The first asks whether every sentence of the content file appears in the page. The second counts the words a style added: nav labels, section names, numerals, its own connective sentences. A style that writes a sentence of its own has stopped being an arrangement of the brief, and the count is where that shows.

    How far apart are they?

    Typefaces in use are the first family of the computed font-family on the heading and the body, read from the live page. “Inks” counts distinct rendered text colours. Radius, motion and weight are the page's own numbers.

    The judgements

    Fidelity is how faithfully the page performs its style: does it keep the conventions that style was built on, and does it keep them where they are expensive. Craft is how good the page is as a page — hierarchy, rhythm, scanning, the form at the end — judged the same way for all nine, including the styles that suit the subject better.

    What this shows

    Method, and what it does not cover

    • Nine pages, plain HTML + CSS + a module that renders the shared content; no build step. Fonts are pinned @fontsource packages from jsDelivr, CSS-only.
    • Each page was screenshotted at 1440 × 900 (hero), full page, and at 390 × 844 in phone portrait, through a RAM-capped Chromium, by tools/shoot.mjs.
    • Every shot records console errors, page errors and failed requests, and an audit of contrast (text colour against its nearest opaque background), unnamed controls, landmarks, images without alt text and language. Those numbers are in the tables above.
    • Each page carries a prefers-reduced-motion block; the count of running animations is measured with and without it in NOTES.md.
    • Not covered: no real user testing, no screen-reader run, no keyboard-only walkthrough, no Lighthouse budget, no cross-browser check (Chromium 152 only), and no judgement of whether the subject should be dressed this way — a grant form in brutalism is a real page for real people, and this study cannot tell you whether it is a good one.
    • Open this folder over http (any static server) — the pages are ES modules and the tables read a generated file, both of which browsers refuse on file://.
