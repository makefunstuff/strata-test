The core flow — log a sighting

Three steps, each a full sheet. Step 2 is the hard one: the app must be useful with one photo, a four-second call and a scribbled note, and it must never guess on your behalf — the confidence is shown, the reasons are listed, and you confirm the bird.

Why the screens look like this

  • Warm paper, one accent, one alarm colour. Moss green is the brand and the primary action; coral is reserved for rare birds, queued records and problems. Everything else is ink on chalk, so a screen full of species names reads as a list, not as a dashboard.
  • Numbers are monospaced, names are not. Counts, times, percentages and coordinates sit in a tabular face so columns of sightings line up; species names stay in the text face because they are words a person reads.
  • Drawings, not photographs. There are no photographs in this build — the birds are silhouettes and the sighting “photo” is a labelled field plate (“drawn from the two photos, not a photograph”). A birding app full of stock photos would be a lie about what this prototype is.
  • Confidence is a claim with receipts. The 91 % on the detail screen is followed by three “+” reasons and one “−”: the call was not used, because the 06:12 recording is a Cetti's warbler. A percentage with no evidence is decoration.
  • Offline is designed, not apologized for. Queued records get a dot in the list, the banner states the outage in minutes, and the map screen says “26 MB · downloaded”. Nothing in the app requires a network to be useful.
  • One handed reach. The primary action is the centre tab button (52 px, thumb-height), the sheet opens from the bottom, back is always top-left, and the tab bar honours env(safe-area-inset-bottom).

What is real in this prototype

  • The tab bar, the FAB, the sheet, the three steps, “none of these”, the count stepper, the save → toast, the row → detail push, the back buttons, the list search, family chips and both sorts, the state switcher, and Escape-to-close are all wired. Open the app and tap through it.
  • The router is query-driven: app.html?screen=log&step=2, ?screen=today&state=offline. That is how every frame on this board is a live app instance, not a picture of one.
  • Touch targets: tab items 44 px, rows 56 px, sheet buttons 44 px, stepper pads 36 px, and the raised FAB 52 px.
  • Motion: sheet 260 ms cubic-bezier, button press 8 % scale, skeleton shimmer; all of it is disabled under prefers-reduced-motion.
  • Accessibility: role="dialog" + aria-modal + focus move on open and focus return on close, Escape closes, aria-pressed on every toggle, role="status" on the toast, rows are buttons with a spoken label (“Wading warbler, 2 birds at sluice pool, 07:20”), the field plate and maps carry role="img" descriptions, and every text/background pair in the audit clears 4.5:1.
