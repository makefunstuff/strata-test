What is in the pack

Cartography, not a map tile cache

Every layer is drawn from survey data, so the map answers the question you are actually asking: where does the fence end, is that a path or a drain, and how steep is the last 200 metres.

  • 10-metre contours, 50-metre index

    Derived from a 1 m LiDAR terrain model, simplified the way a cartographer simplifies: folds kept, spurs smoothed, noise dropped.

  • Whole regions offline

    Download by county, by mountain range, or by drawing a box. A region keeps its contours, paths, buildings, names and elevation together — 38 MB for a national park.

  • GPX, FIT and a route planner

    Drop a file on the app and it draws on the map with elevation profile. Tap to plan: distance, ascent, estimated time on Naismith with a rough-ground factor.

  • Sun, tide and wind on the map

    A sun-angle overlay shows when the ridge leaves shadow, and coastal sheets carry tide times against the footpath that floods at high water.

  • Two days of GPS on 4 %

    Position runs on the coarse motion core, the map stays in memory, the screen is off. Track recording costs about 2 % an hour.

  • The kitchen-table sheet

    Export an A3 or A2 sheet at 300 dpi with grid, scale bar, magnetic variation and a fold line marked, so the paper folds the way it goes in the bag.

Three steps, on the kitchen table

Pack the map on Sunday, walk it on Monday

  1. 1

    Draw the box

    Pan to the ground you want and drag a box. Foldline shows the pack size before you commit — a national park is 38 MB, a whole coast-to-coast is 210 MB.

  2. 2

    Mark the plan

    Import the GPX from the club website, or tap the route in. Add a car park, a water point, the bail-out path. Notes stay attached to the waypoint.

  3. 3

    Print the sheet, then go dark

    Export the folded sheet for the bag, put the phone in airplane mode, and the map is the same map: contours, track log, and a compass that does not need a tower.

Straight answers

Before you download

  • Where does the map data come from?

    Lidar terrain models and open rights-of-way registers, plus our own survey of 6,400 km of path. Every region shows its source and the year it was last surveyed.

  • Does it work with no signal at all?

    Yes. Download, then airplane mode. Nothing in the map, the compass, the track log or the sheet export touches the network; the only thing that does is sync, and you can leave that off.

  • How much phone does a region take?

    A national park is 38 MB of contours, paths, buildings and names; with the 1 m terrain model it is 190 MB. Pathfinder keeps about 60 regions on a 2 GB budget.

  • I already carry paper. Why a phone?

    You keep the paper. Foldline prints the sheet you would have ordered — grid, scale, magnetic variation, fold line — and keeps the track log for the day after.
