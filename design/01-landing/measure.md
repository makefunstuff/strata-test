# 01 · Landing — the hero's numbers, checked against the hero's drawing

Run: `node 01-landing/measure.mjs` (headless Chromium over local HTTP, the same binary as the audit).
The elevation field, the route, the scale bar and the profile are generated by `terrain-boot.js`;
this file reads `window.__sheet` and the rendered page, and refuses a page whose sentences and
whose drawing disagree. The drawn route's own `getTotalLength()` is the ruler — not my arithmetic.

## The scale bar is the ruler

| | value |
| --- | --- |
| the drawn scale bar | 180 user units, labelled `SCALE 1:25 000 0 1 2 km` → 11.11 m per unit |
| the route path's own length | 680 units = **7.6 km** |
| quoted in the legend | 7.6 km ✓ |
| quoted in the sheet's profile plate | 7.6 km ✓ |
| quoted in the sheet's `aria-label` | 7.6 km ✓ |
| derived by the generator | 7.6 km (from 680 units at 11.111 m/unit) ✓ |

Before this round the legend and the plate said **11.4 km** for a route the sheet draws at
**7.6 km** — a 50 % overstatement, invisible to a screenshot, and invisible to
the audit, which compares text against colour and never asks a drawing what it draws.

The copy's own numbers, typed before this round: 812 m, 640 m, 210 m, 11.4 km. The field says
**860 m**, **573 m**, **309 m**, **7.6 km**. The honest number is smaller
than the marketing number, and the sheet is the authority, so the marketing number moved.

## The strip is the plate

The hero strip draws **64** points from **64** field samples — the
same array the sheet's plate draws, so the two strips cannot quote different climbs.
Mapped back through the strip's own scale: **308.9 m** at the car park,
**860.1 m** at the summit, **0.74 m** maximum deviation from the samples.
Caption `7.6 km of route · +551 m · the summit at 860 m · the car park at 309 m`; role `img` with an `aria-label` that states the numbers.

## The fragments are drawings

6 fragments, the least-drawn carrying **4** shapes.

The sheet declares its stroke family on its own groups — `#contours` 0.9, `#index` 1.7, the route
3.4 — and the fragments' computed widths are **0.9px, 1.3px, 1.7px, 3.4px**: the same four
numbers, at a plate 148 units wide instead of a sheet 1 200 wide. A family of nine widths was
the first version of this section; the instrument printed them, so they became four.

Strays from the four: **none**. All labels carry the sheet's halo:
**true**; all are `aria-hidden` because the words beside them are the content:
**true**; labels clipped by a plate edge: **none**.
